Spin-up convergence: whose ruler, and when do we stop?

Only one of our four land models ships a written, quantitative equilibrium standard. This note adopts the CLM5 Satellite-Phenology guide as the common ruler, shows exactly what its diagnostic tools measure, and reads our own CLM5 run against it — which says cycle 3 is not needed.

Prepared 2026-07-12 Models CLM5.0 · JULES vn7.4 · LM4 · Noah-MP v5.2.1 Forcing GSWP3 1981–2010 Status CLM5 cycle 2 running
✓

Decision

Adopt the CLM5-SP guide as the cross-model convergence standard. Our CLM5 run has passed every guide variable except total water storage — the one variable the guide itself flags as slow — after 60 model-years, above the guide's ~50-year target. No cycle 3. Total water storage is treated as a slow reservoir, exactly as the LM4 Sahara deep-water case was.

01

Why CLM sets the standard for all four models

Each model treats spin-up differently, and only one writes down a number you can test against. The rest either bury the test in model code or leave it to judgment.

How each model defines "spun up"
Model Where the criterion lives Judged on Grid coverage required
CLM5‑SP User guide — ~50 model-years from arbitrary IC; most states equilibrate in <10 yr FSH EFLX_LH_TOT GPP H2OSOI TSOI TWS tool default
CLM5‑BGC User guide — AD ~200 yr → final several-hundred yr ecosystem carbon pools + TWS 97% of cells
JULES vn7.4 In the model. &JULES_SPINUP namelist: per-variable tolerance, checked every cycle smcl t_soil c_soil c_veg 100% — every point
LM4 / GFDL No formal tolerance. Carbon-lifetime cap (500 yr) + accelerated substitution + long run (piControl 800 yr+) carbon equilibrium (informal) —
Noah-MP No published HRLDAS convergence criterion practice: soil moisture / temperature stability —

Read down the last two columns: CLM is the only row with a threshold you can compute, a named variable set, and a shipped tool to compute it. JULES is close — it encodes its own test in code — and that test is stricter than CLM's: it demands the tolerance hold at every grid point, with no percentage escape hatch. That matters for our purposes: a state that clears the CLM bar is moving in the safe direction for JULES too. LM4 and Noah-MP leave equilibrium to judgment, so they inherit a borrowed frame — and CLM's is the natural one to borrow.

Adopted common rule. A model is spun up when the between-cycle change in surface fluxes (sensible, latent), near-surface soil moisture, and soil temperature to ~1.5 m is negligible. Total water storage, deep soil water, and deep soil heat are treated separately as slow reservoirs: if they do not affect global or flux diagnostics, the run is called done — the LM4 Sahara precedent.
02

What the diagnostic tools actually check

CLM ships two NCL stability scripts. They share a method — compare a variable's global mean against a target band, year over year — but watch different variables and enforce different thresholds. The SP tool governs our run.

SpinupStability_SP.ncl Satellite Phenology — our run

Used when vegetation is prescribed (no interactive carbon). It asks a physical question: have the land's energy and water states stopped drifting under the cycled forcing? Because there is no carbon to turn over, the answer arrives fast — the guide says under 10 years for most variables.

Six variables it plots against an equilibrium band

FSH — sensible heat flux EFLX_LH_TOT — latent heat flux GPP — gross primary production TWS — total water storage H2OSOI — soil water, layer 8 (~0.80 m) TSOI — soil temperature, layer 10 (~1.36 m)
  1. Take the annual global land mean of each variable, weighted by area × landfrac from the history file.
  2. Plot the series across spin-up years and draw a horizontal equilibrium band (the dotted lines in the guide figures) around the settled value.
  3. A variable is converged once its curve flattens inside the band and stays there. Energy and water fluxes get there first; TWS is expected to lag.
Watch the layer, not the bottom. The tool reads TSOI at layer 10 = 1.36 m, not the model's deepest node. CLM5's ground column runs 25 layers to 42 m of bedrock; that node has a multi-century thermal memory and never settles in an offline run. Judging convergence on it reads a converged model as forever-drifting — a real trap we hit on the first pass.

SpinupStability.ncl Biogeochemistry — reference only

Used when carbon is interactive. Here equilibrium means the ecosystem carbon pools have stopped accumulating, which is slow — soil organic matter in the Arctic turns over on millennial timescales. This tool does not govern our SP run, but it defines the quantitative threshold everyone quotes.

Six variables

TOTECOSYSC — total ecosystem carbon TOTSOMC — soil organic matter carbon TOTVEGC — vegetation carbon TLAI — leaf area index GPP — gross primary production TWS — total water storage

The quantitative equilibrium test

≥ 97% of land cells with |Δ TOTECOSYSC| ≤ 1 gC·m⁻²·yr⁻¹

Equivalently: fewer than 3% of the land surface still in carbon disequilibrium. The last 3% — high-latitude soil carbon — can take 1000+ years to satisfy, and the guide explicitly permits relaxing it by application. This is why a full BGC spin-up is a different order of cost from our SP run, and why we run SP.

Our CLM5 cycle 1, read against the SP guide

Thirty years, cold start, area-weighted global land mean, using the exact variables and layers the SP tool watches. The rightmost column is the mean absolute year-to-year change over the final five years.

Every energy and near-surface water state has settled. The single laggard is total water storage — soil column water plus the unconfined aquifer plus snow — drifting 12.8 mm per year at year 30. That is precisely the variable the guide singles out with "TWS may take a bit longer," for the same reason it lagged in LM4: deep groundwater recharge and snow accumulation have reservoir turnover times far longer than 30 years.

The first read was wrong. An earlier pass measured TSOI at the 42 m bottom node and saw +0.29 K/yr — "diverging." The guide reads layer 10 at 1.36 m, where the drift is 0.11 K/yr and settling. The physics did not change; the layer did. The bottom node's thermal memory is a feature of the 50 m column, not a spin-up failure.
04

What this settles

  • Cycle 3 is not run. The guide target is ~50 years; cycle 1 + cycle 2 is 60. The guide's variables are converged but for TWS, and TWS is the variable the guide itself expects to lag.
  • Cycle 2 gives the final proof. When it finishes, the same-date drift r.2041-01-01 − r.2011-01-01 confirms the flux and soil states have stopped moving between identical-forcing cycles. NO_LEAP makes both restarts land on Jan 1 with no snapshot surgery.
  • TWS carries a caveat, not a veto. Following the LM4 Sahara precedent, a slow reservoir that does not move global or flux diagnostics does not block completion. It is documented, not chased.
  • The rule generalizes. Noah-MP (60 yr) and LM4 (300 yr) are read on the same frame — surface fluxes and shallow soil converged, deep reservoirs noted — so the three models' spin-ups are judged on one consistent standard rather than three ad-hoc ones.
